# Buffett quality screen: 35 Indian listed companies

Research date: 24 November 2026. Latest operating results generally refer to Q2 FY27, the quarter ended 30 September 2026. This is a business-quality screen, not a valuation or buy-price exercise.

## Method and evidence labels

- Each axis is scored from 0 to 5. Total is out of 25.
- `roe_quality`: level, durability and through-cycle consistency of returns, not just the latest ROE.
- `moat`: brand, switching costs, network, scale, cost advantage and evidence from margins.
- `balance_sheet`: leverage, liquidity and capital adequacy. Bank D/E is not used. Bank scores rely on CET1/CAR, GNPA/NNPA and PCR. NBFC scores rely on capital, funding and asset quality.
- `earnings_quality`: cash conversion, tax rate, one-offs and cyclicality. Bank and NBFC cash flow is not directly comparable with owner earnings in an industrial business.
- `management`: capital allocation, dilution, promoter alignment, pledge, related-party conduct and turnaround execution.
- `[File]` means the fact came from `analysis_brief.md`. `[Search]` means it was verified on the web, preferably in an exchange filing, annual report or official results release. `[Derived]` means arithmetic from cited figures. `[View]` means my judgment.
- The file's Yahoo D/E figures are not used literally for banks or NBFCs and are treated cautiously for lease-heavy retailers, hotels and auto-finance groups.

## TOP 10 BY QUALITY

### 1. TCS.NS - 25/25

TCS has the cleanest combination of high returns, cash conversion and governance in the list: official FY22-FY26 ROE was 43.4%, 46.9%, 51.3%, 52.2% and 51.4%, while FY26 free cash flow was Rs 48,013 crore against PAT of Rs 49,210 crore. [Search] Its scale, mission-critical relationships and 24.0% Q2 FY27 operating margin support a strong moat, and the 71.77% promoter holding is unpledged. [Search] This is a Buffett-type compounder, although constant-currency growth of only 2.8% in Q2 and AI-related price deflation mean it is no longer a high-growth story. [Search]

### 2. HDFCBANK.NS - 22/25

HDFC Bank survived the pandemic and absorbed the large HDFC Ltd merger while keeping asset quality unusually strong: Q2 FY27 GNPA was 1.11%, NNPA 0.37%, credit cost 44 bps, CET1 17.1% and CAR 18.9%. [Search] ROE fell from 18.7% in FY16 to 14.4% in FY26 because the merged mortgage book diluted returns, not because the franchise broke. [Search] The bank has no promoter group, so promoter pledge is not applicable; the main risks are deposit competition, CASA mix and NIM rather than solvency. [Search]

### 3. ICICIBANK.NS - 22/25

ICICI's decade is a useful test of resilience: standalone PAT fell from Rs 98.01 billion in FY17 to Rs 33.63 billion in FY19 after the corporate-credit and governance crisis, then recovered to Rs 501.47 billion by FY26. [Search] Q2 FY27 GNPA improved to 1.20% from 1.58% a year earlier, NNPA to 0.30%, PCR was 75%, CET1 16.01% and CAR 16.68%. [Search] It has no promoter group and a strong retail and deposit franchise, but bank earnings remain exposed to credit costs, NIM and regulatory decisions. [Search]

### 4. INFY.NS - 22/25

Infosys remains a high-return, cash-rich franchise: FY26 ROE was 31.6%, free cash flow Rs 31,259 crore against PAT Rs 29,440 crore, and the company completed a Rs 18,000 crore buyback at Rs 1,800 per share. [Search] Its policy is to return about 85% of cumulative five-year free cash flow through dividends and buybacks, which is shareholder-friendly capital allocation. [Search] The 13.90% promoter holding is unpledged, but FY27 revenue guidance was cut to 1%-2% constant currency and Q2 growth was only 1.5%, so the quality is high but the growth momentum is not. [Search]

### 5. DIVISLAB.NS - 21/25

Divi's has a real technical moat in complex APIs, custom synthesis and backward integration, supported by a 42.6% Q2 FY27 EBITDA margin and zero borrowings. [Search] The founding family holds 51.88% with no pledge, and the long-term profit record is positive despite a weak FY24. [Search] It is not a full 25 because H1 FY27 operating cash flow was only Rs 384 crore against PAT of Rs 2,029 crore as inventory absorbed Rs 1,585 crore, while capex guidance remains about Rs 2,000 crore. [Search]

### 6. HCLTECH.NS - 21/25

HCLTech combines a 24.65% trailing ROE, 41.2% trailing ROIC, an 18.2% Q2 EBIT margin and Rs 24,503 crore of net cash. [Search] Its engineering services and HCL Software provide more differentiation than a pure labor-arbitrage IT model, and management says advanced AI growth is offsetting AI-related deflation. [Search] The official September filing shows 60.88% promoter holding and no pledge; the main watch items are the 11.2% year-on-year decline in HCLSoftware revenue and execution risk in large AI and cloud programs. [Search]

### 7. HEROMOTOCO.NS - 20/25

Hero's brand, dealer network and scale in entry-level motorcycles create a durable moat, and the balance sheet is conservative with file D/E of only 6.01%. [File] The 34.72% promoter holding is unpledged. [Search] Q2 standalone revenue rose 21% but PAT only 5%, with EBITDA margin falling to 13.4% from 15.0%, while consolidated PAT growth of 41% included a Rs 397 crore associate gain, so the underlying earnings are less strong than the headline. [Search]

### 8. KOTAKBANK.NS - 19/25

Kotak has one of the strongest capital positions in the list, with Q2 CET1 of 21.4%, CAR of 21.9%, GNPA of 1.04% and NNPA of 0.24%. [Search] Its deposit, wealth-management, insurance and securities franchises are a credible moat, and the 25.87% promoter holding is unpledged. [Search] The score is held back by a lower 11.65% quarterly ROE and NIM compression to 4.31%, which show that even a good bank does not have unlimited pricing power. [Search]

### 9. TITAN.NS - 19/25

Titan's Tanishq, Titan and other brands, together with a large retail network, justify a high moat score and the file's 45.27% ROE is excellent. [File] The 52.90% promoter holding is unpledged. [Search] Q2 reported PAT of Rs 2,393 crore was flattered by a Rs 536 crore gold customs-duty benefit and a Rs 671 crore put-option remeasurement gain, while lease-heavy debt and rising finance costs mean reported profit is not the same as owner earnings. [Search]

### 10. TRENT.NS - 19/25

Trent has compounded revenue from Rs 1,814 crore in Q2 FY23 to Rs 5,788 crore in Q2 FY27, and Q2 operating EBITDA grew 37% with EBIT margin improving to 11.6% from 10.5%. [Search] Westside and especially Zudio have scale, sourcing discipline and own-brand differentiation, and the 37.01% promoter holding is unpledged. [Search] The caveat is that fashion like-for-like growth was only mid-single digit, so much of the growth comes from new stores, leases and working capital rather than proven pricing power. [Search]

## WHAT I COULD NOT VERIFY

- I did not rebuild a complete audited 5- or 10-year cash-flow and ROE series for every company. The deepest history was checked for TCS, Infosys, HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, Titan, Tata Chemicals, Tata Steel, Divi's, Muthoot, TVS Motor and the Tata demergers. The other names rely more on current results and shorter histories.
- Promoter pledge is verified to the latest quarterly filing, generally September 2026, not to today. Natco's post-quarter rights-issue pledge is separately verified. A zero in a quarterly filing is not proof that no later pledge event occurred.
- I did not obtain a complete lease-adjusted net-debt bridge, pension liability schedule or off-balance-sheet commitment note for all 35. Retail, hotel and auto-finance scores can change materially on a lease-adjusted basis.
- Bank and NBFC free cash flow is not economically comparable with industrial FCF. Loan growth consumes cash; deposit and wholesale-funding inflows are not owner earnings. I used asset quality, capital, funding costs and credit costs instead.
- I did not verify every bank's full sector concentration, restructured-book detail and stressed-asset pool. TMB's ECL transition gap and South Indian Bank's technical write-offs deserve follow-up.
- I did not verify post-September cure rates for Muthoot's and Manappuram's Stage 2 gold loans. Their Q2 jump is verified; its permanence is not.
- IT AI revenue definitions are not consistent: TCS reports annualized AI revenue, Infosys reports a quarterly share and HCLTech reports quarterly advanced-AI revenue. I did not invent a common AI revenue comparison or quantify AI headcount reduction.
- TMPV's pre-demerger history is not directly comparable with the post-demerger PV-plus-JLR company. I did not present a synthetic 10-year TMPV ROE series.
- I did not exhaustively review related-party transactions, promoter-entity personal debt, contingent liabilities or auditor qualifications for every company. The specific IndusInd and TMB issues were verified, but the absence of a finding elsewhere is not a clean audit opinion.
- Some data portals conflict with official filings. The output uses official totals where available, notably HCLTech's 60.88% total promoter holding and Manappuram's nonzero pledge.
