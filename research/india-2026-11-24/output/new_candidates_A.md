## THE 3 WORTH ADDING

### 1. LICI.NS - Rs 407.30

Single number: approximately 0.65x Indian Embedded Value [CALC: Rs 515,234 crore market capitalization divided by Rs 797,378 crore Indian Embedded Value at 30 September 2026].

LIC is India's dominant life insurer, with a 58.77% share of first-year premium income in H1 FY2027, an enormous agency franchise and a powerful brand. Its moat is distribution and trust rather than manufacturing scale. The balance sheet has no borrowings, solvency was 235% versus 213% a year earlier, Q2 value of new business margin improved to 21.82%, and net profit rose 20.12% to Rs 12,076 crore [SEARCH]. Government holding fell from 96.50% to 90.00% after an offer for sale, with no promoter pledge. The market is paying only about 0.65x actuarial embedded value and 8.3x trailing earnings, below the four valid listed-year averages. For the Rs 3,500 budget, 8 whole shares cost Rs 3,258.40, leaving Rs 241.60 cash [CALC].

Biggest risk: LIC earns a material part of profit from its investment portfolio, so an equity-market drawdown can hit earnings and book value together; government divestment and agency productivity are secondary overhangs. The three auditor emphasis matters around family pension, excess management expenses and additional pension liabilities also deserve monitoring, although the audit opinion itself was unmodified.

### 2. COALINDIA.NS - Rs 382.00

Single number: 6.94% trailing dividend yield [SEARCH].

Coal India is a Maharatna CPSE operating 310 mines and supplying a power sector that takes roughly 80% of dispatches. Its moat is the domestic resource base, mine scale, logistics and energy-security role; it is not a company with unrestricted pricing power. It is net cash, with no borrowings problem and no promoter pledge, and government holding is 61.13% after a 2% offer for sale. Q2 FY2027 net profit rose 53% to Rs 6,516 crore, but the operating margin fell to 19.2% from 26.1% in the preceding quarter, so the earnings direction is not one-way. At 7.1x earnings and 1.8x book versus a 2.56x five-year year-end PB average, the shares offer a cash yield without balance-sheet risk. Nine whole shares cost Rs 3,438.00, leaving Rs 62.00 cash [CALC].

Biggest risk: regulated domestic coal pricing, weaker production/offtake and the long transition away from coal can reduce earnings and the dividend; the pending Mahanadi Coalfields IPO also introduces valuation and supply overhang.

### 3. CREDITACC.NS - Rs 983.80

Single number: 1.79% gross NPA, the best asset-quality reading in ten quarters [SEARCH].

CreditAccess Grameen is a rural microfinance lender serving about 44.98 lakh borrowers through 2,326 branches. Its moat is the loan-officer distribution network, local collection intelligence and customer data, not a license or a product that competitors cannot copy. The Q2 FY2027 recovery is substantive: AUM grew 19.5% to Rs 30,946 crore, GNPA fell to 1.79%, NNPA to 0.59%, PAR 90+ to 1.11%, and CRAR was 26.9% with Tier 1 at 26.2%. Profit reached Rs 502 crore and management guided to 21-24% ROE. The shares trade at 10.0x earnings versus a 24.67x five-year year-end average and 1.8x book versus 2.81x. Promoter holding is 63.83% with no pledge, although the promoter sold 2.28% in August 2026. Three whole shares cost Rs 2,951.40, leaving Rs 548.60 cash [CALC].

Biggest risk: microfinance credit stress can return quickly, while a 50-basis-point lending-rate cut in October 2026, possible further cuts and the promoter's reported desire to exit create earnings and ownership overhangs.

### NATIONALUM.NS - WATCH IT

Business and moat: NALCO is an integrated bauxite-alumina-aluminium-power producer with captive feedstock and power. Its cost position and integration are the moat; it remains a price taker in aluminium.

ROE: Current ROE is 32.00%, above its 22.64% five-year average, but FY2023 and FY2024 ROE of 11.17% and 14.45% show why the business is not a stable 30% compounder through a full cycle.

Balance sheet: September 2026 borrowings were zero, with net debt/equity of -0.49x and a current ratio of 4.07x. H1 operating cash flow of Rs 4,371 crore exceeded H1 profit of Rs 3,838 crore. Capital work in progress was Rs 6,412 crore.

Ownership and governance: Government of India holds 51.28%; promoter pledge is 0.00%. The prompt's claim that NALCO is a Vedanta subsidiary is wrong. Company and Ministry of Mines material identify it as a Navratna CPSE under the Ministry of Mines.

Auditor/regulatory: The Q2 limited-review opinion was unmodified, but auditors drew attention to unrecognized revenue from two Rajasthan wind plants pending fresh PPAs; the matter is in Rajasthan High Court and revenue has not been recognized since 1 April 2019. Management's Q2 call described local resistance and tree-clearing problems for the Pottangi bauxite mine road in Odisha. I could not verify the alleged "Pugaar" Jharkhand land-valuation dispute or any amount.

Valuation: 7.7x PE versus a 10.01x five-year average; 2.2x PB versus 1.98x. H1 profit rose 54%, but Q2 EBITDA margin fell 658 bps sequentially to 44.5% as input and power costs rose.

Why a long-term investor should not own it: A low multiple can coincide with peak aluminium margins, and the unresolved PPA, mine-access and Rs 6,412 crore CWIP issues limit predictability. WATCH until mine access is resolved and mid-cycle margins are clearer.

### SCI.NS - AVOID IT

Business and moat: SCI is India's largest shipping company by capacity, operating roughly one-third of India's shipping tonnage across tankers, bulk carriers, liners and offshore services. Scale and government relationships help, but freight rates are global and cyclical.

ROE: Current ROE of 21.31% is well above the 12.25% five-year average, consistent with a strong tanker cycle rather than a permanent step-change.

Balance sheet: Gross borrowings were Rs 2,517 crore at September 2026, D/E 0.24x and net debt/equity 0.21x. Current ratio was 3.23x. The company recognized a Rs 240.19 crore provision against its loan to India LNG Transport Company (No. 3), and Screener flags Rs 4,099 crore of contingent liabilities.

Ownership and governance: Government holding is 63.75%, pledge 0.00%. As a CPSE, capital allocation, vessel investment and dividend policy are exposed to government priorities.

Auditor/regulatory: I did not verify a current auditor qualification or SEBI action. The ILT-3 provision and geopolitical impact are disclosed business risks, not an audit qualification.

Valuation: 5.9x PE versus 8.45x history; 1.2x PB versus 0.98x. Q2 consolidated profit rose 205.8% to Rs 577.76 crore, but EBITDA margin fell to 36.4% from 45.3% in Q1 and the effective tax rate was only about 2%.

Why a long-term investor should not own it: A low PE at high tanker earnings is the classic cyclical trap; normalized freight rates, fleet renewal and government-directed capital allocation can all reduce the apparent bargain.

### LICI.NS - OWN IT

Business and moat: LIC combines India's largest life-insurance franchise, a vast agency network and sovereign-linked brand trust. Distribution density and customer trust are durable advantages, although private insurers compete aggressively on products and technology.

ROE: Current ROE is 40.57%. FY2023-FY2026 listed-year ROE was 65.70%, 48.67% and 40.77%, so the return is moderating but remains high. The extreme FY2022/FY2023 pre-IPO percentages are not comparable because the equity base was tiny.

Balance sheet: No borrowings, solvency of 235% and Indian Embedded Value of Rs 797,378 crore. The investment portfolio is both the earnings engine and the principal market-risk exposure.

Ownership and governance: Government holding is 90.00% after the 2026 OFS, pledge 0.00%. The divestment improves float but creates periodic supply risk.

Auditor/regulatory: V. Sankar Aiyar & Co. and Mukund M Chitale & Co. gave an unmodified review conclusion. Three emphasis matters relate to regulator-approved amortization/replenishment of family pension, excess management expenses and additional pension liabilities. Remaining balances were Rs 3,712.13 crore, Rs 1,205.02 crore and Rs 912.85 crore respectively. A GST litigation notification appeared in March 2026, but the exposure is not verified.

Valuation: 8.3x PE versus 9.71x over four valid listed years; 3.1x PB versus 5.21x. The derived 0.65x IEV is the most interesting valuation measure, while recognizing that embedded value depends on actuarial assumptions.

Why a long-term investor should not own it: Investment losses can compress profit and book value at the same time, and the government can keep selling stock. I still prefer owning it at this price because the franchise, capital and valuation are unusually strong together.

### EMMVEE.NS - AVOID IT

ROE: Current ROE of 53.05% is supported by real post-IPO profits, not merely a one-quarter gain. However, FY2023 and FY2024 ROE were only 5.53% and 18.69%, and FY2025's 104.60% came off a small equity base. There is no five-year listed record.

Balance sheet: Gross debt was Rs 1,108 crore, net debt about Rs 726 crore and D/E 0.25x at September 2026. Current ratio was 3.14x. TTM free cash flow was negative Rs 941 crore after Rs 14,300 crore of capex. FY2026 operating cash flow was only Rs 200 crore against Rs 1,082 crore profit.

Ownership and governance: Promoters hold 80.03%, pledge 0.00%. The IPO listed on 18 November 2025 at Rs 217. Free float was 138.25 million shares and 20-day average volume 2,004,431 shares, roughly Rs 52 crore of daily value [CALC]. This is adequate for a small retail order but still small-cap liquidity. The company secretary/chief operating officer resigned in July 2026.

Auditor/regulatory: The 2026 AGM proceedings state that statutory and secretarial audit reports had no qualification, observation or adverse remark. I did not verify a material SEBI action. Customer concentration and detailed order-book economics were not verified.

Valuation: 12.5x PE and 4.1x PB, against only one valid historical year at 13.92x and 4.07x. The absence of history is a risk, not evidence of cheapness.

Why a long-term investor should not own it: High ROE does not equal high owner earnings while expansion consumes cash and the product lacks durable pricing power. Revisit after free cash flow turns positive and customer concentration is fully disclosed.

### LLOYDSME.NS - AVOID IT

Business and moat: Lloyds produces iron ore, sponge iron/DRI and pellets, with mining, MDO services and a new slurry pipeline that lowers freight cost. Resource access and logistics create a real cost moat, but product prices remain commodity-linked.

ROE: Current ROE is 40.41%, versus a 25.25% five-year average that includes a -28.70% loss year in FY2023. Recent returns are strong but partly reflect rapid expansion and operating leverage.

Balance sheet: September 2026 net debt was Rs 19,917 crore, gross borrowings Rs 23,005 crore, D/E 1.23x, current ratio 0.82x and CWIP Rs 18,174 crore. TTM free cash flow was negative Rs 6,051 crore. Ownership and governance: Promoters hold 61.61%. The official September 2026 shareholding pattern reports 14.43% of promoter shares pledged, 60.50% under non-disposal undertakings and 74.93% total encumbered. It also reports 15 million locked-in promoter shares. This is the key governance risk, not the misleading "0% pledge" extracted by one data service.

IPO/lock-in correction: The premise that Lloyds is a recent IPO is not supported. A company ESOP document says its IPO was in 2000. I could not verify a recent IPO issue price or the origin/expiry of the 15 million locked-in shares.

Auditor/regulatory: Todarwal & Todarwal LLP gave an unmodified limited-review conclusion, but emphasized standalone trade receivables of Rs 468.71 crore, including Rs 321.44 crore of NTPC HPC wage-reimbursement recoverables. CRISIL also flags legacy promoter litigation and large guarantees/acquisition leverage.

Valuation: 18.2x PE versus 35.16x over four valid years; 5.9x PB versus 9.17x. The shares only look cheap if rapid growth continues and debt is successfully reduced.

Why a long-term investor should not own it: Debt-funded capex, negative free cash flow, an audit emphasis and 74.93% encumbered promoter holding create too many simultaneous ways to lose money.

### COALINDIA.NS - OWN IT

Business and moat: Coal India is the dominant domestic coal producer, with resource scale, 310 mines and a strategic role in power supply. Its moat is cost, reserves and infrastructure, while coal pricing remains politically sensitive.

ROE: Current ROE is 28.33%, below the 44.12% five-year average but still strong. Every year in FY2022-FY2026 generated a return above 27%.

Balance sheet: Net debt/equity is -0.44x and D/E only 0.12x. Cash generation and a high dividend payout are central to the investment case.

Ownership and governance: Government holding is 61.13% after the June 2026 OFS, pledge 0.00%. Dividend policy is strong historically but remains a government/board decision.

Auditor/regulatory: I did not verify a current auditor qualification or SEBI action. Relevant policy risks include domestic coal pricing, e-auction realizations, mine clearances and the Mahanadi Coalfields IPO filed in November 2026. FinMinutes flags a 10.7% year-on-year fall in depreciation as a question, not proof of an accounting change.

Valuation: 7.1x PE versus 6.86x history; 1.8x PB versus 2.56x. Trailing dividend yield is 6.94%. FY2026 production and offtake fell 1.7% and 2.4%, and Q2 FY2027 EBITDA margin fell sharply, so this is not a no-risk bond substitute.

Why a long-term investor should not own it: Coal demand and margins can weaken, and political pricing can cap upside. I still prefer owning it because net cash, domestic demand and the yield provide a margin of safety unavailable in most commodities.

### JSWSTEEL.NS - AVOID IT

ROE: The supplied 31.9% current ROE is reported, not normalized. FY2026 included an Rs 18,051 crore gain from loss of control/slump sale of the BPSL steel business. FY2026 reported PAT was Rs 25,508 crore versus normalized PAT of about Rs 8,698 crore. Underlying ROE is closer to low double digits, and FY2023-FY2025 ROE was only 12.22%, 4.32% and 6.11%.

Balance sheet: Net debt fell to Rs 44,482 crore at September 2026, net debt/EBITDA was about 1.25x and D/E 0.62x. Deleveraging is genuine, but FY2027 capex guidance is Rs 22,000-24,000 crore.

Ownership and governance: Promoters hold 44.30%. Trendlyne reports 12.25% of promoter holdings pledged, equal to 132,730,505 shares or about 5.42% of total equity [CALC]. Some promoter entities have much higher individual pledge percentages.

Auditor/regulatory: I did not verify a current audit qualification or SEBI action. The one-time BPSL gain and a 13.9% year-on-year fall in depreciation are the important earnings-quality questions.

Valuation: Headline 11.3x PE versus 31.51x history is optical; forward PE is 19.72x. PB of 2.8x is slightly above the 2.67x history, and the shares are at 84% of their 52-week range.

Why a long-term investor should not own it: The market is paying a near-high price for a cyclical recovery while trailing earnings contain a large non-recurring gain and capital expenditure remains heavy.

### IEX.NS - WATCH IT

Business and moat: IEX operates India's premier electricity exchange, historically with about 85% market share. More participants create liquidity and better price discovery, a strong network-effect moat.

ROE: Current ROE is 38.61%, against a 41.90% five-year average. FY2022-FY2026 returns stayed in a tight 39.42%-49.02% range, the best durability in this group.

Balance sheet: IEX is debt-free and net cash equal to 1.32x equity. September 2026 investments were Rs 2,244 crore against Rs 1,460 crore equity.

Ownership and governance: There is no promoter; pledge is not applicable. Professional management and a broad institutional/public shareholder base are positives.

Auditor/regulatory: Walker Chandiok & Co. LLP gave an unmodified Q2 review with no emphasis of matter. However, the company disclosed two contra-trade code violations on 30 October 2026, a warning and Rs 1,230 disgorgement. Separately, SEBI barred eight individuals over Rs 173 crore of alleged insider trading in IEX options ahead of the July 2025 CERC market-coupling decision; the reviewed reports identify external traders and CERC officials, not an IEX company charge.

The larger issue is CERC market coupling. Draft regulations were issued in April 2026; final rules were still pending at the October call. Management estimated 1.5-2 years of implementation after final notification. Q2 electricity volume was 39.7 billion units, up 12.7%, with RTM at nearly 42% of volume, and net profit rose 12.9% to Rs 139 crore. Other income was still 20% of PBT.

Valuation: 18.6x PE versus 38.69x history; 6.7x PB versus 15.28x. The discount is substantial, but PB remains high in absolute terms because the exchange earns extraordinary returns on equity.

Why a long-term investor should not own it yet: Market coupling can transfer the network/liquidity advantage to a common operator and weaken transaction pricing power. Wait for final rules and evidence that IEX retains share and margins.

ROE: Current ROE is 21.74%, up from a 14.57% five-year average. The improvement is real, but 7.0x net debt/equity magnifies equity returns.

Balance sheet: September 2026 borrowings were Rs 165,159 crore, loan book about Rs 1.86 lakh crore and net worth Rs 22,959 crore. CRAR was 32.55%, gross credit-impaired assets 0.71%, net 0.04%, and provision coverage 93.76%.

The same report says HUDCO did not have the requisite number of independent directors for April-September 2026, committee composition was non-compliant, and NSE/BSE had levied fines disclosed as contingent liabilities. Valuation: 7.1x PE versus 9.86x; 1.4x PB versus 1.39x. H1 profit rose 31.5%, but leverage and governance outweigh the apparent discount.

Why a long-term investor should not own it: A policy lender with 7.0x leverage, a disputed large receivable and a disclosed board-compliance failure is not the safe cheap PSU the headline multiple suggests.

### CREDITACC.NS - OWN IT

Business and moat: CreditAccess provides joint-liability-group microfinance to women and is expanding into rural retail finance. Its branch network, loan-officer intelligence and long customer relationships create a distribution moat, though banks and other MFIs can compete.

ROE: Current ROE is 19.73%, above the 13.97% five-year average but below the 24.77% peak in FY2024. Q2 FY2027 ROE was 23.2% and management guided to 21-24% for FY2027.

Balance sheet: Debt was Rs 25,128 crore and equity Rs 8,919 crore, D/E 2.8x. CRAR was 26.9%, Tier 1 26.2%, liquidity Rs 3,502 crore and undrawn sanctions Rs 6,759 crore. GNPA was 1.79%, NNPA 0.59%, PAR 90+ 1.11% and collection efficiency about 98%.

Ownership and governance: Promoter holding fell from 66.21% to 63.83% after a 2.28% block sale in August 2026; pledge is 0.00%. Reports suggest the promoter wants to exit, but no completed change of control was verified.

Auditor/regulatory: I did not verify a current auditor qualification or SEBI action. RBI's microfinance framework limits eligible households to annual income up to Rs 3 lakh and total monthly repayment outflows to 50% of household income. Pricing and conduct rules constrain yield, while weather and local over-indebtedness remain credit risks.

Valuation: 10.0x PE versus 24.67x history; 1.8x PB versus 2.81x. Profit recovery is partly from lower provisions, so the important test is whether NII and collections stay strong as lending rates fall.

Why a long-term investor should not own it: A renewed microfinance credit cycle, further rate cuts or a promoter exit can reverse the recovery. I still prefer owning it because capital, liquidity and the valuation provide a buffer that the market is ignoring.

### ENGINERSIN.NS - WATCH IT

Business and moat: Engineers India is a government-owned engineering consultancy and project-management company with decades of hydrocarbon, infrastructure and energy experience. Technical reputation and order execution are the moat; project timing is not.

ROE: Current ROE is 29.68%, up from a 19.01% five-year average and 7.93% in FY2022. Returns have improved consistently, but other income matters.

Balance sheet: No borrowings, net cash equal to 0.63x equity and a current ratio of 1.32x. Financial strength is excellent for a project business.

Ownership and governance: Government holding is 51.32%, pledge 0.00%.

Auditor/regulatory: I did not verify a current audit qualification or SEBI action. Q2 order book reached a record Rs 20,421 crore, but H1 revenue fell about 9%. Q2 reported profit rose 137.3% while core profit rose 48.6%; other income was 35% of PBT.

Valuation: 18.7x PE versus 18.75x history; 5.0x PB versus 3.17x. The shares are at 89% of their 52-week range.

Why a long-term investor should not own it: Record orders do not justify a premium PB while revenue is shrinking and a large share of profit comes from non-core income. WATCH for order conversion and margin durability.

### MAHABANK.NS - WATCH IT

Business and moat: Bank of Maharashtra is a public-sector bank with roots in small business, traders and self-employed borrowers. Deposit reach and government support are useful, but it lacks strong pricing power.

ROE: Current ROE is 23.29%, versus 18.90% over five years. The bank had losses in FY2017-FY2019, so this is a turnaround, not a perpetual 23% compounder.

Balance sheet and asset quality: Q2 GNPA was 1.25%, NNPA 0.12%, provision coverage 98.53%, CRAR 18.51%, Tier 1 16.37% and CET1 15.61%. Capital and asset quality are strong. Domestic CASA fell to 46.47% from 50.35%, overseas advances jumped to Rs 18,095 crore from Rs 888 crore a year earlier, and borrowings rose to Rs 47,890 crore from Rs 24,924 crore.

Ownership and governance: Government holding is 73.60%, pledge 0.00%. Nidhu Saxena assumed MD and CEO charge on 27 March 2024; the management change is verified and precedes the improved results.

Auditor/regulatory: I did not verify a current audit qualification or SEBI action. Screener flags Rs 50,756 crore of contingent liabilities. The main risks are deposit competition, rapid overseas expansion and the rate cycle.

Valuation: 9.4x PE versus 8.02x history; 2.0x PB versus 1.35x. At 96% of the 52-week range, much of the turnaround is already priced.

Why a long-term investor should not own it: The bank is performing well, but the price assumes continued flawless execution while CASA mix and overseas expansion add risk. WATCH for a pullback or evidence that overseas growth is profitable and well funded.

## THE 3 MOST TRAP-LIKE

1. LLOYDSME.NS - The 18.2x PE looks cheap against 35.16x history, but net debt is Rs 19,917 crore, TTM free cash flow is negative Rs 6,051 crore, the auditor emphasized Rs 321.44 crore of NTPC reimbursement receivables, and 74.93% of promoter shares are pledged or under non-disposal undertakings. Growth is being purchased with leverage and encumbrance.

2. JSWSTEEL.NS - The 11.3x PE is not a normalized multiple. An Rs 18,051 crore one-time BPSL gain lifted FY2026 reported PAT to Rs 25,508 crore, versus normalized PAT of about Rs 8,698 crore. Forward PE is 19.72x, the shares are near a 52-week high, and FY2027 capex is Rs 22,000-24,000 crore.

3. HUDCO.NS - The 7.1x PE and 1.4x PB resemble a value stock, but net debt/equity is 7.0x, auditors emphasized a Rs 743.16 crore project receivable, and the review report discloses a failure to have the required independent directors plus exchange fines. Cheap PSU financing is not automatically safe owner earnings.
