## Tensions reconciled (the three memos disagree)

1. Quality and valuation memos like TCS, INFY and HDFCBANK. The sector memo ranks IT LAST because of AI pricing deflation. Resolution: quality plus valuation trump a 6-12 month sector call for a 5+ year holder, but only at a real discount. TCS at 17.2x versus its own 26.9x five-year average on 45.6% ROE, and INFY at 14.7x versus 24.2x on 30% ROE, are genuine discounts, not traps. I still keep IT to one slot and do not overweight it.

2. Screener and valuation memos like MUTHOOTFIN and TMB on valuation (8.4x and 8.6x earnings). The quality agent found Muthoot Stage 2 at 12.5% (from 1.1%) under the new RBI gold-loan rules, accrued interest nearly doubled to ~Rs 46,000 crore, and TMB facing a Rs 1,037 crore ED penalty overhang. Resolution: I reject both. Cheapness on a single number is not enough when the earnings base itself is in question. Low PE on deteriorating asset quality is a value trap, not a bargain.

3. Sector memo favours ICICIBANK and TVSMOTOR. TVSMOTOR at Rs 4,528 is not affordable (one share is 130% of the budget). ICICIBANK at Rs 1,585.50 is affordable but crowds out diversification: one share plus TCS leaves almost no room, and two bank shares in the same sector is not real diversification. HDFCBANK is cheaper on its own history (2.1x versus 2.8-2.9x five-year P/B) and is the bank slot I kept.

## THE HONEST CAVEAT

Is Rs 3,500 split this way sensible at all, versus waiting and investing a larger sum? My genuine view: splitting Rs 3,500 across 2-4 names is barely better than not investing, and in some respects worse, because the fixed DP charge of Rs 15.34 per scrip per exit is 0.44% of the whole capital per name and the round trip is 0.99% to 1.88% before any market movement. You are paying a structural tax of about 1-2% for the privilege of owning two to four pieces of paper. On top of that, a 75% single-stock weight (Portfolio 1) is not diversification in any meaningful sense; it is a concentrated bet dressed up as a portfolio, and a 30% fall in TCS alone takes a fifth of your capital.

The sensible alternatives, in order: (a) if you can add even Rs 1,500-2,500 more within the next few months, wait and invest Rs 5,000-6,000 in one or two names, which at least halves the cost drag per rupee; (b) if this Rs 3,500 is genuinely all you have and all you will have for a year, put it in a Nifty 50 index fund or a liquid fund instead, since one index unit gives you 50 stocks for a single DP charge and zero stock-picking risk, which is a better deal than four hand-picked names at this size; (c) if you are determined to own individual stocks, Portfolio 1 is the least bad option, but go in knowing that TCS at 75% of your money is a bet, not a portfolio, and that you should not add a third name just to feel diversified, because three names at Rs 3,500 means roughly Rs 1,100 each and the friction is 1.4% plus the same concentration problem. My definite recommendation: if you will not add money soon, take option (b); if you will add money within 6 months, take Portfolio 3 and let it grow into Portfolio 1. Do not take Portfolio 2 at this size unless you specifically want the learning experience of tracking four companies, because the 1.88% friction and four exit charges are not justified by the spread you actually get.

## WHAT WOULD MAKE ME CHANGE

1. TCS constant-currency growth re-accelerates above 5% and AI revenue share keeps rising while the PE stays below 20x. That would strengthen, not weaken, the Portfolio 1 core; I would add to TCS rather than trim. Conversely, if TCS operating margin falls below 22% for two consecutive quarters, I would exit TCS and replace it with HDFCBANK plus cash.
2. Muthoot Stage 2 falls back below 5% and credit cost normalises below 0.5% for two quarters running, with no new RBI gold-loan enforcement action. That would lift the rejection and make MUTHOOTFIN (8.4x earnings, 31.6% ROE) the first name I add in Portfolio 3's accumulation phase, displacing INFY as the second slot.
3. ICICIBANK falls to or below Rs 1,450 (about an 8.5% dip) without a deterioration in GNPA or CET1. At that price the own-history and peer-relative case becomes clearly better than HDFCBANK's, and I would swap HDFCBANK for ICICIBANK in Portfolio 1 and Portfolio 3.
